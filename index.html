Four Lines: Where My Family Actually Comes From

Every family has stories. I wanted to know which ones were true. So I ran the records down, all four of my grandparents' lines, and this is what the paper actually says.

Quick rules on how to read this. Nothing here is copied off somebody's Ancestry tree and taken on faith. Every claim is graded honestly: Proven means an actual record backs it, a death certificate, a church register, an obituary, a census page. Probable means the evidence converges but the one clinching document hasn't been pulled yet. Family story means that's what got passed down and we haven't tested it. Where the records contradict the family story, I say so.

Credit where due: I didn't read thousands of archive pages myself. I used AI to sweep the digitized records, newspaper archives, cemetery databases, and church registers, then verified the hits by eye. Some of what's below took a machine reading records no human had looked at in 80 years.

Quintanilla

My mother's father's line

Documented link by link through the Mexican registers, back to the Spanish families who founded Monterrey, Mexico in 1596, twenty-four years before the Mayflower. Three hundred years in Mexico, then to San Antonio. Three brothers in World War II, including a medic hit by a V-1 buzz bomb in Belgium.

Paper trail: to the 1596 founders of Monterrey. The deepest line in the family.

Martinez

My mother's mother's line

A Tejano family, rooted at Mission Espada on the San Antonio River. One branch traces to the Canary Islanders who founded San Antonio in 1731; another came up from Mexico a century later. Close to the whole span of Tejano history in one tree.

Paper trail: 1731 founding families (probable) down through the mission church registers.

Flynn

My father's father's line

Famine Irish out of Curraheen, near Tralee in County Kerry. Andrew Flynn landed in 1850 with nothing. Three generations later his grandson was Boston Latin, Harvard, and a company vice president. Then the story breaks in one week in October 1931.

Paper trail: the Tralee parish registers, documented to Michael Flynn and Johanna Crean in the 1780s.

MacColl

My father's mother's line

A tailor's son left Glasgow in 1882 and ran one of Rhode Island's big textile mills for fifty years. The family came off the Isle of Mull in the Scottish Highlands. And through the American wife who married in, the Kimbarks, this line reaches back to Richard Warren on the Mayflower in 1620.

Paper trail: Glasgow to 1813, and a documented Mayflower descent through the Kimbark branch.

The one-paragraph version

My dad's side is famine Irish who clawed up in Boston, plus Scottish mill money out of Glasgow by way of the Isle of Mull, with a documented Mayflower line (Richard Warren) coming in through the American Kimbark marriage. My mom's side is a Tejano family from the Mission Espada community on the San Antonio River, one branch tracing to the Canary Islanders who founded San Antonio in 1731, and on the other side a line documented back to the founders of Monterrey, Mexico in 1596, three centuries in Mexico before San Antonio. Four lines, four countries, and every one of them ran through a war, a famine, or a border to get here.
