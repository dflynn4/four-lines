## 1. The bond (image 942) — printed form, filled in by hand
> "Know all Men by these Presents, That we **David Kim[be]rgh & William [Sinse]baugh** all of
> **Shawangunk in the County of Ulster** are held and firmly bound unto **Adam Kimbergh of the town
> of Shawangunk in the county of Ulster, INFANT UNDER THE AGE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS**, in the sum of
> **two hundred and fifty Dollars** current money of the State of New-York … Sealed with our seals.
> **Dated the fourth day of Nov[ember] … one thousand eight [hundred] and sixteen** … and of our
> Independence the forty first.
>
> WHEREAS the above bounden **David Kimbergh**, by order and authority of the Surrogate of the county
> of Ulster, on the day of the date hereof, is allowed [and] appointed **a Guardian** of the above
> named [Adam Kimbergh] during his minority, pursuant to an Act entitled 'An act authorising
> Surrogates to appoint Guardians for Infants,' [passed] 5th April 1809 …
>
> The Condition of this Obligation is such, That if the above bounden **David Kimbergh** shall
> faithfully in all things discharge the duty of a Guardian to the above named **ADAM C. KIMBERGH**
> according to the laws of this state, and render a true account of his guardianship … then this
> above obligation to be void."

**Signed and sealed** (read off the image, two red wax seals still attached):
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**David Kimbergh** — a fluent autograph signature, not a mark
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**William Sinsebaugh**
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Witness: **[Daniel] Brodhead Jr.**

## WHAT THIS PROVES

1. **The name.** Adam C. Kimbark signed himself, and the Ulster Surrogate wrote him down, as
   **"Adam C. Kimbergh."** Until now the German spelling was only inferred from the 18th-century
   Montgomery church records and from Jones's *More Palatine Families*. Here it is in **his own
   lifetime, in his own county, in a court record.** The chain Gimberg → Kimbergh → Kimbark now runs
   unbroken through a single named man. **PROVEN.**

2. **The date fits him exactly.** Adam C. Kimbark was born 10 Oct 1798 (family memorial, 1880).
   On 4 Nov 1816 he was **18 years and 25 days** — squarely "an infant under the age of twenty-one."
   New York's 1809 act let a minor **over fourteen choose his own guardian by petition**, which is
   precisely the procedure used here. There is no competing Adam C. Kimbergh.

3. **His father was dead by 4 November 1816.** A Surrogate appointed a guardian only for a minor
   whose father was deceased (a living father is guardian by nature). So the father died between
   Adam's birth (Oct 1798) and Nov 1816, in or near the town of Shawangunk, Ulster County.

4. **David Kimbergh of Shawangunk is a close blood relative** — court-appointed guardian, literate,
   solvent enough to post a $250 bond. On the Montgomery baptismal register **David Kimbergh &
   Mary Crist** baptised Matty (1789), Sally (1797) and Mary Ann (1800). He is Adam's uncle or elder
   brother — the guardian is never the father, so David is NOT Adam's father. **PROVEN as guardian;
   CANDIDATE as to exactly which relation.**

5. **He had property.** A guardian bond is taken at roughly double the minor's estimated personal
   estate; $250 implies an inheritance of order $125 in 1816 money — a real legacy, not nothing.
   That legacy is the reason Adam went to the Surrogate at all, and it is the thread that leads to
   his father's estate file.

6. **Two years later** — 10 Dec 1818, in the same town — Adam married Sarah Masten at the Shawangunk
   Reformed Dutch Church. The 1816 guardianship and the 1818 marriage bracket the moment this family
   stopped being Kimbergh and started being Kimbark.

## WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE
The father's **name**. The petition that carried it is torn off. Everything above stands without it.

## THE NEXT DOCUMENT
The **Ulster County Surrogate's minute / order book for 1816** — the clerk's own register of
appointments, which records "on the petition of Adam C. Kimbergh, infant son of ____, deceased,
David Kimbergh appointed guardian." That is now the one document that would name Adam's father
outright. Also worth pulling: any Ulster **administration bond** for a Kimbergh of Shawangunk dying
c. 1799-1816.
